But since the Franciscan pharmacy is still operating in Dubrovnik, we have something rather extraordinary to compare against. Its documented preparations include rose cream, rosewater, herbal creams and ointments. The pharmacy has confirmed that ingredients and recipes changed over the years, although the dates and reasons for individual substitutions still need to be established. Much of the earlier archive was destroyed in the earthquake and fire of 1667.

What makes this particularly interesting is that the proposed manuscript readings contain corresponding ingredient vocabulary. The current recipe decoder gives specific rose or rosewater candidates on f9v, f105v and f115v, alongside oil and water vocabulary. I also compared the proposed vocabulary against Ragusan government and trade records from 1359 to 1364. That gives us specific ingredients, folios and historical records to examine together. Establishing a shared recipe history still requires the individual formulas and preparation sequences to line up.

That doesn't independently establish that the manuscript belonged to the Franciscans. It gives us a surviving pharmacy, a documented medieval pharmaceutical economy, and a manuscript whose proposed vocabulary and working structure can be tested against both.

The full research, folio translations and validation are available here: https://github.com/denoflore/ZFD
